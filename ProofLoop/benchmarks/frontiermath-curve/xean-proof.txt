## w3-1/n1

Let C be the projective plane curve x³y+y³z+z³x=0 over F5. Write F=x³y+y³z+z³x. Its derivatives are F_x=3x²y+z³, F_y=x³+3y²z, F_z=y³+3z²x. A common zero of all derivatives with x=0 would have z=0 by F_x and then y=0 by F_z, which is impossible in projective space. Cyclic symmetry handles the other vanishing coordinates. If xyz≠0, put a=x³y, b=y³z, c=z³x. The derivative equations multiplied by x,y,z give 3a+c=a+3b=b+3c=0. Over F5 the first two give c=2a and b=3a, while the last then gives 4a=0, impossible. Thus C is smooth, and its genus is (4−1)(4−2)/2=3.

Over an algebraic closure choose a primitive seventh root ζ and set A[x:y:z]=[ζx:ζ⁴y:ζ²z]. Each of the three terms of F is preserved, so A is an automorphism of order 7. Every nonidentity power A^j has three distinct diagonal eigenvalues and consequently fixes on C precisely the three coordinate points [1:0:0], [0:1:0], [0:0:1]. Since the action is tame, Riemann–Hurwitz for C→D=C/⟨A⟩ gives 2·3−2=7(2g(D)−2)+3(7−1), hence g(D)=0.

Use H=H¹_et(C over algebraic closure,Q3), of dimension 6. Standard pullback/transfer for the quotient (7 is invertible in Q3) identifies H^⟨A⟩ with H¹_et(D,Q3)=0. The cyclotomic polynomial Φ7 is irreducible over Q3: 3 has multiplicative order 6 modulo 7. Thus the only possible nontrivial irreducible Q3-representation of the cyclic order-7 group has dimension 6. It follows that over an algebraic closure of Q3, A has precisely its six nontrivial seventh-root eigenvalues, each with multiplicity one.

Let Fr be geometric or arithmetic Frobenius acting on H with the conventional choice that the trace formula is N_n=5^n+1−Tr(Fr^n); either convention can be arranged compatibly by taking the corresponding cohomological Frobenius. Frobenius conjugates A to A^5 or A^(5 inverse), depending on convention. Both powers act transitively on the six nontrivial seventh roots because 5 has order 6 modulo 7. Therefore Fr cyclically permutes the six one-dimensional A-eigenspaces. It follows that its characteristic polynomial is T⁶−c and Fr⁶=c·I for some scalar c. The characteristic polynomial has integer coefficients, hence c is an integer; the Weil absolute-value theorem gives |c|=(sqrt(5))⁶=125. Thus c=125 or −125, and N6=5⁶+1−6c=15626−6c.

Because 7 divides 5⁶−1, the root ζ and automorphism A are defined over F_(5⁶). Its action on C(F_(5⁶)) has orbits of size 1 or 7, and its only fixed points are the three F5-rational coordinate points. Hence N6≡3 (mod 7). If c=125, then 15626−750≡1 (mod 7); if c=−125, then 15626+750≡3 (mod 7). Consequently c=−125, so the Frobenius polynomial is T⁶+125. As Fr¹⁸=(Fr⁶)³=(−125)³I, the requested number of nonzero solutions up to F_(5¹⁸)^× scaling, equivalently the number of projective points, is N18=5¹⁸+1−6(−125)³=3,814,708,984,376. There are no unresolved gaps beyond the explicitly cited standard algebraic-geometric theorems.
